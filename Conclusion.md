# Conclusion

## What we did
We tested LearnLoop, a web-based learning management system with student and teacher roles, AI-generated notes and quizzes. We prepared a test plan, designed 46 test cases using equivalence partitioning, boundary value analysis, decision tables, state transition testing and error guessing, executed them manually, documented the defects found, and automated checks with Pytest and Selenium.

## Results
- 46 test cases executed: 42 passed and 4 failed.
- 4 defects found: 2 High, 1 Medium, 1 Low.
- Automated tests: Pytest 25 passed of 25; Selenium 2 passed of 2.

## Key findings
- Input validation was strong at the boundaries: the password minimum of 8 characters and the quiz score thresholds behaved correctly.
- The weak points were less obvious: a password of only spaces passes validation, anyone can register as a teacher, and a second open quiz overwrites the first one.
- Error guessing found more defects than the designed cases. Planned techniques confirm that expected behaviour works, while exploratory testing exposes what the specification left out.

## What we learned
- Combining several techniques gives better coverage than any single one.
- UI automation needs explicit waits, because the browser does not finish loading instantly after a click.
- A clean pass result on designed cases does not mean the application is free of defects.

## Recommendations
1. Reject passwords that are blank after trimming spaces.
2. Do not let visitors choose the teacher role; require approval by an administrator.
3. Tie each quiz to its own identifier instead of storing one quiz per session.
4. Accept only Beginner, Intermediate or Advanced as the level.

## Limitations
- Performance, load and security testing were out of scope.
- The quality of the content generated by Gemini was not evaluated.
